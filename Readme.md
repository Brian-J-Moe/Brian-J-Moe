

## Brian J. Moe, PhD

#### *Quantitative Ecologist \| Bayesian Statistical Modeling \| R Development*

<br>

I am a quantitative fisheries ecologist whose research focuses on the spatial ecology, life history, and population dynamics of elasmobranchs. My current research examines the space use, movement patterns, and habitat use of juvenile smalltooth sawfish within the Charlotte Harbor critical habitat area. A major focus of this work is understanding refuge attraction and temporal variation in the spatial ecology of young-of-year sawfish and their interactions with young-of-year and juvenile bull sharks occupying the same nursery habitats.

I also have longstanding interests in elasmobranch life-history dynamics, including individual growth and mortality, and in understanding how variation in these processes influences population productivity, resilience, and recovery. My research makes extensive use of Bayesian statistical methods to integrate multiple sources of biological information, quantify uncertainty, and improve inference for data-limited populations and species.

I develop statistical models and reproducible computational tools for ecological and biological research, with particular expertise in population dynamics, animal movement, spatial ecology, and fisheries.
